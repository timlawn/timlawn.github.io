Excitation/inhibition (E/I) imbalance is implicated in the pathophysiology of psychosis, but the extent to which this is evident before the onset of illness is unclear. Here, we investigated E/I-related brain dynamics using the Hurst exponent (HE) derived from resting-state MRI in two longitudinal cohorts of individuals at clinical high risk for psychosis (CHR-P): PSYSCAN (190 CHR-P individuals and 115 controls) and Accelerating Medicines Partnership® Schizophrenia (826 CHR-P individuals and 241 controls). Both cohorts showed lower whole-brain HE in CHR-P individuals than in controls at baseline, with the most prominent differences observed in the somatomotor network and thalamus. In both cohorts, normative receptor distributions significantly predicted the spatial pattern of HE differences, with consistent contributions from norepinephrine, serotonin, and opioid receptors. In PSYSCAN, 21 CHR-P individuals transitioned to psychosis during follow-up. There was preliminary evidence that subsequent transition to psychosis was associated with a longitudinal reduction in HE in the right inferior parietal cortex and that baseline regional HE showed above-chance prediction of subsequent transition at the individual level. Together, these findings suggest that vulnerability to psychosis is associated with reduced HE, consistent with less persistent brain dynamics and increased excitability.
